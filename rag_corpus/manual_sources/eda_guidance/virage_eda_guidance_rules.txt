# ViRAGE EDA guidance rules

source_kind: eda_guidance
guidance_topic: eda_overview
output_target: eda

Rule: A broad EDA request should not produce only one decorative chart. It should cover complementary aspects of the dataset: distributions of important numeric variables, group comparisons if categorical variables are present, missingness, outliers, correlations, and temporal trends when date or time fields are available.

Rule: For a primary distribution check, prefer a histogram, density-like view, boxplot, or compact distribution chart that makes spread, skewness, and potential outliers visible. Avoid hiding the distribution behind a single aggregate value.

Rule: For group comparison during EDA, show both central tendency and spread. For scientific or small-sample data, prefer boxplots, strip plots, or summaries with uncertainty instead of a mean-only bar chart.

Rule: For correlation analysis, use scatter plots or correlation heatmaps only when the relevant numeric variables exist. Avoid plotting identifiers, row numbers, or high-cardinality codes as analytic measures.

Rule: For temporal trend analysis, preserve chronological ordering, show gaps or irregular sampling when visible, and avoid connecting unrelated categories as if they were continuous time.

Rule: Missingness analysis should show which fields contain missing values and whether missingness is concentrated in specific variables or groups. Do not silently ignore missing fields in an EDA request.

Rule: Outlier analysis should make extreme values visible without letting them compress the rest of the chart into a small unreadable region. Consider axis-domain choices, log scale, or separate diagnostic views when appropriate.

Rule: High-cardinality categorical fields should not be shown as overcrowded bar charts. Use top-N ranking, aggregation, grouping, or a warning that the field is not suitable for direct categorical plotting.

Rule: EDA charts should be diagnostic and honest rather than publication-polished only. They should reveal data quality problems, unusual distributions, and suspicious values even if the result is less visually simple.

Rule: If the user asks for article-ready figures, combine EDA coverage with publication constraints: readable labels, compact layout, no tooltip-only information, and clear axis units when available.
